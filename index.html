02 / Method

How a chef-engineer actually works.

01 · Mise en place

Get in the kitchen

I show up. Walk the line. Understand the actual workflow, not the org chart. Find the friction.

02 · Recipe

Design the tool

Sketch the smallest possible thing that removes the most pain. Test it on real prep, not slides.

03 · Service

Ship & train

Deploy with the team using it. Train in their language. Adjust on the fly during real service.

04 · Plate-up

Hand off & scale

Document so it survives without me. Ship to the next outlet. Repeat.

03 / About

Twenty years at the pass. Now shipping software for the people still working it.

I've cooked at Michelin tables, opened restaurants, and run kitchens in hotels, campuses, and high-volume cafés. The tools the industry builds for chefs were not built by chefs. So I started building them.

CIA-trained. Cooked at Gordon Ramsay at The London (2 ★) and The Modern at MoMA (1 ★). Ran the kitchen at the Knickerbocker Hotel in Times Square. Opened Wallflower (NYT review · Hungry City).

Ran kitchens for Restaurant Associates inside the Food at Google program (2021–2024) and now at Shopify NYC — high-volume, exec-visible corporate dining where "food as amenity" is the whole job. Along the way I became one of the first chefs inside Restaurant Associates to operationalize ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini — building job aids, allergen logic, prep math, and signage automation in production kitchens.

Today I ship culinary tools — Aila, ForkRecipe, Emojirecipe, FoodML — in the evenings. Looking for the right team to do this at scale.
